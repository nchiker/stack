## Decision for this bounded pass

Keep the platform renderer and independently implement the necessary fixes in Java. This avoids a new native build, package-size increase or first-use download. The cost if this decision is wrong is continued maintenance of a heuristic detector; reconsider a pinned, bundled OpenCV processing component when a broader real-photo corpus establishes remaining failure patterns. This review is not a comparative runtime benchmark of the shortlisted apps.

Useful techniques are separation of detection, geometry, lighting and tone; multiple candidate thresholds; denoising an estimate rather than the original text; and checking geometry against image evidence. We do not transplant upstream transform chains or constants. In particular, sharpening/difference-of-Gaussians can amplify texture; global binarization can discard faint gray marks. Neither is a justified cure for the observed defects without fixed-source measurements.

Stack's processing sequence remains:

1. Detect on a bounded sample and propose corners only when boundary evidence supports them. Preserve manual/full-frame fallback.
2. Decode with crop-aware sampling, then apply a four-point perspective transform. Keep the existing 4,096-pixel input and 1,600/3,200-pixel preview/export bounds.
3. Estimate local paper illumination separately from source stroke samples. Denoise the estimate and use grayscale morphological closing (maximum followed by minimum) to avoid spreading bright outliers/shadow boundaries as a maximum alone does.
4. Normalize source luminance by local paper, then apply a continuous contrast curve with a small noise allowance and a wider retained gray range. No additional sharpening or binary threshold is applied.
5. Render derived previews/PDFs. Original files, saved corners, rotation and color selections remain authoritative and editable.

Perspective rectification does not flatten curved pages. Original color and the two color presets remain necessary for photographs, diagrams, colored/dark paper and detail that monochrome normalization cannot distinguish from paper noise. Verification and remaining quality limits are recorded in [the quality evidence](STACK_SCANNER_QUALITY.md).

## Requested curved-paper extension

During implementation the user explicitly requested both perspective straightening and bend/curl correction. The latter is a separate feature. Matt Zucker's [page-dewarping writeup](https://mzucker.github.io/2016/08/15/page-dewarping.html) and [MIT-licensed implementation](https://github.com/mzucker/page_dewarp/blob/master/page_dewarp.py) illustrate a fuller text-span/cubic-sheet/camera optimization approach, requiring Python, SciPy, OpenCV and Pillow. No source or examples were copied; that toolchain was not adopted.

The independently authored `PageFlattening` is deliberately narrower: on an already cropped, upright page it correlates lower-stroke row profiles across nine vertical strips, requires agreement between the upper/lower halves, and fits a small quadratic vertical displacement. Minimum correlation/variance, strip coverage, residual, curvature and displacement bounds reject unsupported estimates. A small bitmap mesh applies the inverse bend while pinning the page borders. This straightens gently bowed horizontal text; it does not estimate a 3D surface, undo horizontal foreshortening, reconstruct hidden text, or flatten arbitrary folds/book gutters.

Flattening is off by default, including for all existing pages and drafts. **Crop & rotate → Flatten** previews a supported estimate; **Use flattening → Apply** stores the per-page choice. The same pipeline handles document viewing and edited PDF export. Removing the choice restores source geometry. A failed automatic crop also keeps the currently displayed manual corners, rather than resetting them to the full frame. The cost of this conservative choice is that some curved pages receive no correction and require a flatter capture. Broader photographed-page validation remains open.
